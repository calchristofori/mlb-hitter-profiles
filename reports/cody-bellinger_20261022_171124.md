# Cody Bellinger — LF, New York Yankees · Age 30 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Bellinger is a contact-and-launch hitter who rarely strikes out and puts the ball in play at productive angles to his pull side. That makes him a high-average bat with below-average power, because his exit velocity doesn't let his lift turn into much damage.

## Expected stats
His overall level is a shade above average. His xwOBA (house model; expected wOBA from contact quality, strikeouts and walks) was .340, and it has risen two seasons in a row. How he gets there defines him. His xBA (house model) of .270 is well above average, while his xISO (expected extra bases per at-bat) of .149 is in the bottom half of qualified hitters and fell in 2026. In 2024 and 2025 his actual results ran ahead of his contact quality. In 2026 the two lined up, so his dip in wOBA reads as luck evening out, not a weaker hitter.

## Batted-ball profile
He wins through efficient launch, not exit velocity. His EV90 of 102.8 is below average, though it has risen two seasons in a row. Most of his batted balls come off at useful angles: his Sweet spot% of 38.7% ranks 13th of 135, and his launch spread is tighter than average. He also pulls the ball in the air (Pulled Air%) well above the league rate. The 2026 change was a flatter swing. His median launch angle dropped to 16.0, his fly balls fell and his popups rose, and he hit the opposite field less often. That bought more sweet-spot contact and cost carry, which shows in his below-average Barrel% and falling xISO.

## Plate discipline
Contact is the anchor. His Whiff% of 20.0% is in the top quarter of qualified hitters, and his K% of 15.8% is well below the league rate. He is selective rather than aggressive: he swings at strikes less often than average, and that rate has fallen two seasons in a row. His chase rate is roughly league average, which leaves his IZ Swing − Chase (zone swing rate minus chase rate) in the bottom third. The patience did pay off in walks. His BB% rose for a second straight season to 10.5%, above average.

## Platoon profile
The Yankees used him as an everyday player and leaned into it. His 35.6% share of PA against lefties was well above the 25.1% average for left-handed hitters and near the top of that group, and it rose from 2025. His contact holds up against lefties, with the same xBA and fewer whiffs than against righties. His power does not, and his xwOBA against them was .315 against .354 versus right-handers. He reads as an everyday bat whose damage comes almost entirely against right-handed pitching.

## Vs. pitch type
Breaking balls are his strength. His .350 xwOBA against them ranks in the top fifth, and pitchers have responded: his breaking share fell two seasons in a row and is now in the bottom fifth of qualified hitters. They have shifted to fastballs, whose share rose in 2026. He makes contact against fastballs but rarely barrels them, ranks in the bottom quarter in Barrel% on the pitch and takes more in-zone fastballs than most. Offspeed produced his weakest contact (.262 xwOBA), just outside the bottom quarter on a modest sample. His whiff rate against offspeed is good, so the problem there is quality of contact, not missing. None of this adds up to a clear hole.

## Synthesis
Bellinger's value comes from two skills working together. He makes contact far more than most hitters, and when he connects, the ball tends to leave at a productive angle toward the pull side. That combination produces a strong xBA with a low strikeout rate, and growing patience has added walks on top. The ceiling is set by his exit velocity. Without top-end impact, his pulled air balls become line drives and warning-track flies more often than barrels. That keeps his xISO below average and his overall output only slightly above it.

His biggest vulnerability is that same lack of impact, and it shows most against lefties and against fastballs he puts in play without driving. His passivity on in-zone fastballs suggests pitchers can get ahead of him with heat. The direction is mixed. His EV90 and xwOBA have both risen two years running, so there is no sign of a slowing bat in his exit velocity. But his Whiff% and Pulled Air% both moved the wrong way in 2026, his zone swing rate has fallen two straight seasons, and his sprint speed has slipped to league average. At 30, the flatter swing likely reflects an approach change toward contact over lift. Still, that cluster is worth watching.
