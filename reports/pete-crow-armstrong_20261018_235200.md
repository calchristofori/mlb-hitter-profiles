# Pete Crow-Armstrong — CF, Chicago Cubs, Age 24, Bats L / Throws L

## Bottom line
Crow-Armstrong drives value by pulling the ball in the air more than any qualified hitter. That shape is backed by above-average power and elite speed, and it produced a 13th-ranked xwOBA in 2026. His vulnerability is swing-and-miss: he chases breaking and offspeed pitches at bottom-quarter rates, and his K% sits in the 20th percentile.

## Profile
**He is a pull-side, air-ball power hitter whose value comes from batted-ball shape more than raw force.**

- **Performance:** His xwOBA (house model), which estimates the value of his contact from exit velocity and launch angle plus his actual walks and strikeouts, was .383. The qualified average was .338, putting him 13th of 135 (91st percentile). Power carries it: his xSLG (house model) of .516 ranks in the 94th percentile, while his xBA (house model) of .264 ranks in the 65th.
- **Power:** His EV90 of 106.6 is above the 104.6 average (75th percentile). That is a good power ceiling, not an elite one.
- **Batted-ball tendency:** His Pulled Air%, the share of batted balls hit in the air to the pull side, was 34.8% against a 19.4% average. That ranked first of 135.
- **Discipline:** His IZ Swing − Chase, in-zone swing rate minus chase rate, was .360, below the .374 average (37th percentile).
- **Speed:** His sprint speed of 29.5 ft/s ranks 6th (96th percentile).

Interpretation: he gets more out of moderately-plus force than most hitters because so much of his hard contact goes into the air toward right field.

## Underlying vs. results
His wOBA of .406 ranked 2nd, ahead of his .383 xwOBA (+.023), with BABIP jumping +.051 to .327. Elite speed may account for part of that gap, but expect some regression toward the expected line; park effects cannot be checked with this data.

## Skill breakdown
**Contact quality is strong across the board.**

- Hard-hit% was 48.8% (85th percentile), Barrel% was 12.9% (90th) and Sweet spot% was 38.3% (87th).
- Hard-hit% has risen from 36.8% to 41.9% to 48.8% over three seasons.

**He lives in the air.**

- His LA median of 22.0 is far above the 14.7 average.
- His GB% of 31.3% is well under the 41.0% average, and his FB% of 33.6% is above the 27.8% average.
- His LA SD of 27.1 is slightly tighter than average (66th percentile), so the lift is fairly controlled.
- The cost is popups: PU% has risen from 6.0% to 9.5% to 11.7%.
- Pull% was 54.9% against a 44.1% average, and Oppo% was only 18.3%. He rarely works the other way.

**His 2026 approach changed markedly.**

- IZ Swing% fell 12.1 pts to 69.1%, still above the 66.8% average.
- Chase% fell 8.6 pts to 33.1%, but it still ranks 98th of 135 (28th percentile).
- BB% jumped to 11.4% (78th percentile) from 4.5%.
- Whiff% was 26.3% (35th percentile) and K% was 25.8% (108th of 135).

Interpretation: he became more selective, but he cut in-zone swings as much as chases, so his swing-decision score actually dipped. The walks are the clear gain, and the strikeouts remain a cost.

## Platoon splits
He is an everyday bat whose damage is concentrated against right-handers.

- **Vs RHP (496 PA):** .406 xwOBA, 107.3 EV90 and 24.6% K%.
- **Vs LHP (230 PA):** .334 xwOBA, 105.0 EV90 and 28.3% K%. His .387 wOBA ran well ahead of expected (+.053).

Interpretation: against lefties he is closer to a league-average hitter whose results were flattered.

Interpretation: the plan against him is soft stuff off the plate. Breaking or offspeed pitches left in the zone get punished.

## Strengths and concerns
**Strengths**
- League-leading Pulled Air% (34.8%) paired with a 90th-percentile Barrel%.
- Elite damage on offspeed (.439 xwOBA, 1st) and breaking pitches (.360, 90th percentile).
- Speed (29.5 ft/s, 96th percentile) and a much-improved BB% (11.4%).

**Concerns**
- K% of 25.8% (20th percentile).
- Breaking-ball Chase% of 40.4% (9th percentile).
- Fastball Whiff% of 23.0% (bottom quarter), along with a weaker line against LHP (.334 xwOBA).

## Outlook
The trend points firmly up. EV90, Pulled Air% and Whiff% are all moving the right way, and at 24 there is no sign of a slowing bat. The one thing to watch is whether the 2026 chase and walk gains hold. If they do, the profile is durable. If they slip back, his 2025 discipline numbers suggest the strikeouts and the gap between results and expected stats would bite harder.
