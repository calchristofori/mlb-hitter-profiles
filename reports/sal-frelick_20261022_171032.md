# Sal Frelick — RF, Milwaukee Brewers · Age 26 (2026) · Bats L / Throws R

## Top line
Frelick is a bat-to-ball, ground-ball hitter who rarely swings and misses but rarely hits the ball hard. His value depends on contact and speed turning into batting average, and in 2026 that contact produced too little average or power to make him an above-average bat.

## Expected stats
His xwOBA (house model: what his contact, walks and strikeouts should have produced) was .283, well below the qualified average of .338, and he ranked near the bottom of qualified hitters in each of the two prior seasons. In 2024 and 2025 his xBA (house model) sat near league average, which carried the profile, but in 2026 it slipped below average to .238. His xISO, the extra bases his contact should produce per at-bat, was .090, far below the qualified average of .175, though it has risen two seasons in a row. His 2025 wOBA ran well ahead of his xwOBA (+.034), so that year's results overstated his skill. His 2026 results roughly match his contact.

## Batted-ball profile
His contact is soft and low. His EV90 of 101.1 trails the qualified average of 104.6, and it has sat near the bottom of the league in prior seasons. His LA median of 7.5 degrees has fallen two seasons in a row, so about half his batted balls are on the ground. A wide launch spread means the balls he does lift are inconsistent, some driven and some popped up, rather than reliably squared. In 2025 he briefly pushed his Pulled Air% (balls hit in the air to the pull side) slightly above average, but it fell back to 14.4% in 2026. Hard-hit% and Barrel% both stay far below average, so there is little damage to offset all the grounders.

## Plate discipline
Contact is his standout skill. His Whiff% of 10.1% is far below the qualified average of 23.9%, and his strikeout rate of 9.9% follows from it. He also swings less often than average, both at strikes and at pitches off the plate. His low chase rate therefore partly reflects selectiveness across the board, not a hitter who attacks the zone and lays off everything else. His IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) is below average but has risen two seasons in a row. His walk rate has crept up two seasons in a row but remains below average.

## Platoon profile
In 2026 he faced lefties about as often as other left-handed hitters, which means he played every day. That continues the role he held in 2025, after being kept away from lefties somewhat in 2024. His 98 PA against left-handers in 2026 are too few for any read on that split. Against righties he was the same hitter described above (.276 xwOBA). He played as an everyday bat, but his split against lefties remains unproven.

## Vs. pitch type
Fastballs are where the profile shows its limits. He makes contact easily but does little damage, with a .292 xwOBA against them, well below the qualified average of .368. Pitchers seem to know it. His fastball share rose to 66.0% in 2026, well above average, which reads as pitchers challenging him without fear of the long ball. Against breaking balls he barely misses and his results sit somewhat below average. Against offspeed pitches he almost never whiffs, but there are too few plate appearances ending on them for a read on results.

## Synthesis
Frelick wins by making contact. He swings less than most hitters, rarely chases, and almost never misses when he does swing, so nearly every plate appearance ends with a ball in play. What that ball looks like is the problem. Modest exit velocity, a launch angle that keeps sinking, and an inconsistent launch spread produce mostly grounders and weak air contact. His speed, which remains above average, helps those grounders find holes. That combination yields a batting average that sat near league average in 2024 and 2025 and almost no extra bases. When xBA slips, as it did in 2026, nothing else holds his xwOBA up.

His defining strength is bat-to-ball skill. His biggest vulnerability is the lack of damage, which pitchers exploit by pumping fastballs at him. The trend is mixed rather than declining. EV90 ticked up, xISO has risen two seasons in a row, and his whiff and strikeout rates keep falling, so the bat does not look like it is slowing. The concern is direction: the pulled, lifted contact he found in 2025 did not hold, and his launch angle keeps dropping, which pushes him further toward a ground-ball, singles-dependent profile. His sprint speed has also slipped slightly two seasons in a row, which matters for a hitter this reliant on grounders.
