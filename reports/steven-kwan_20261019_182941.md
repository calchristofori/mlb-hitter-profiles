# Steven Kwan — CF, Cleveland Guardians · Age 28 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Kwan is an extreme contact-and-patience hitter who almost never misses, rarely chases and sprays consistent line drives to all fields without any exit velocity behind them. He drives value by getting on base through a high batting average and a rising walk rate, not by doing damage.

## Expected stats
His 2026 xwOBA (house model; expected wOBA from contact quality, strikeouts and walks) of .332 sits just under the qualified average. The shape of that number is lopsided. His xBA (house model) of .280 ranks in the 87th percentile, while his xISO (expected extra-base power per at-bat) of .067 ranks 134 of 135. That xISO is the lowest of his five seasons and well off his 2024 peak of .106. His xwOBA still rose from 2025 because walks filled the gap. Results matched expected almost exactly, so his 2026 line reflects his actual skill.

## Batted-ball profile
Kwan's contact is the most repeatable in baseball and among the weakest. His EV90 of 95.3 ranked last among qualified hitters, and his Hard-hit% did too. Yet his launch-angle spread was the tightest in the league, and his Sweet spot% ranked first. He lifts the ball on a productive line-drive plane with almost no topped or popped balls. He does not pull much. His Pulled Air% (balls in the air to the pull side) is in the 13th percentile, and he uses the whole field. The trend is the concern: EV90 fell 2.9 mph and Hard-hit% fell 8.9 points from 2025, both to career lows.

## Plate discipline
He is selective to the point of passivity, and his contact is elite. His Chase% and Whiff% both rank 2nd of 135, which keeps his K% among the league's lowest. In 2026 he also swung at far fewer strikes. His IZ Swing% dropped 7.3 points to 51.5%, well under the qualified average. That pushed his IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) down to the 16th percentile, but it also lifted his BB% to 13.3%. He is taking strikes, not expanding the zone.

## Platoon profile
He is the same hitter against both sides, slightly less effective against left-handers. Lefties pushed his Whiff% and K% up and his xwOBA down to .320, below his .337 against righties. Against lefties his actual results lagged his expected stats. The batted-ball profile and discipline barely change by side, so he reads as an everyday bat rather than a platoon candidate.

## Vs. pitch type
Kwan has no true hole by pitch group, but he does no damage on any of them. Fastballs are where his production lives, with an xwOBA right at the qualified average. Breaking balls and offspeed pitches give pitchers the most success, as both sit below average on xwOBA without falling into the bottom quarter. His whiff rates on all three groups are near the top of the league. Pitchers have responded by throwing him fastballs 67.9% of the time, well above the 55.2% average and up from 2025. That likely reflects how little risk a strike carries against his exit velocity, and he takes in-zone fastballs at a low rate.

## Synthesis
Kwan wins by putting the ball in play on a perfect plane and refusing to swing at bad pitches. Elite contact, elite launch consistency and near-zero chase produce a high xBA, and his walks have become a second pillar. His defining strength is that almost nothing he does at the plate is wasted: few whiffs, few popups, few chases.

His vulnerability is that none of it carries any damage. With the league's lowest top-end exit velocity, pitchers can pound the zone with fastballs. His 2026 answer was to take more strikes and walk more, which raised his xwOBA even as his power nearly disappeared. The bigger signal is that EV90 and Pulled Air% both fell this year. Rising Sweet spot% and Oppo% suggest part of that is a deliberate all-fields approach. Still, a 2.9 mph drop in EV90 at 28 reads as more than an approach change. His value now rests almost entirely on batting average and walks, which leaves little margin if the contact softens further.
