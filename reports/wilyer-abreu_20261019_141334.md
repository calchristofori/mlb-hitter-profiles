# Wilyer Abreu — RF, Boston Red Sox · Age 27 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Abreu is a lift-and-barrel slugger. His force and contact are both close to league average, but an efficient launch turns that contact into well-above-average extra-base damage. He drives value through power, and in 2026 he paired it with a shorter, more all-fields approach that has steadily cut his strikeouts.

## Expected stats
His xwOBA (house model; expected wOBA based on contact quality, strikeouts and walks) of .354 ranks 37th of 135 qualified hitters, against a .338 average. It has climbed each season from .330 in 2024. He gets there through power, not average. His xBA (house model) of .252 sits at the league norm. His xISO (expected extra bases per at-bat) of .224 ranks 25th, well above the .175 average. His .340 wOBA trails his expected mark, so his results slightly undersell his contact.

## Batted-ball profile
Abreu's damage comes from angle more than force. His EV90 (90th-percentile exit velocity) of 105.3 is only modestly above the 104.6 average, and it is down from 107.5 in 2025. He lifts the ball consistently, though. His median launch angle is 21.0 degrees against a 14.7 average, and his launch spread is tighter than average (66th percentile). That combination turns ordinary force into a 12.1% Barrel% that ranks 24th, with fly balls well above and grounders well below league norms.

The 2026 shape changed. His Pulled Air% (pulled fly balls, liners and popups as a share of batted balls) fell from 25.7% to exactly the 19.4% league average. His pull rate dropped below average and his opposite-field share rose, while Sweet spot% climbed to 36.3%. That reads as more line-drive, all-fields contact at some cost to pulled-air damage. Hard-hit% also slid for a second straight year, to 43.5%.

## Plate discipline
His approach is now league average across the board. His in-zone swing rate, Chase% and Whiff% all sit near the norm. His IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) of .381 is close to the .374 average. The story is the trend. Whiff% fell from 29.6% in 2024 and held in 2026, and K% has dropped from 28.0% to 20.7%, matching the average. His 10.4% BB% sits a tick above. He swung at fewer strikes than in 2025, so the strikeout gains are not coming from added aggression.

## Platoon profile
Abreu is an everyday bat: his xwOBA is .359 against lefties and .352 against righties. He reaches those numbers differently. Against lefties he is a contact hitter, with a 16.6% K% and a .267 xBA, but only a 103.0 EV90 and more grounders. Against righties the power plays, with a .237 xISO and a 13.0% Barrel%, though his results there lag his expected mark the most.

## Vs. pitch type
Breaking balls are his best matchup. He posts a .363 xwOBA (12th of 135), a better-than-average whiff rate and a 14.2% Barrel% (15th). Fastballs are the soft spot. His fastball xwOBA of .358 is a bit under average, but his 23.7% fastball Whiff% ranks 108th, in the bottom quarter. His fastball Chase% has also risen each season, from 18.2% in 2024 to 28.1%. Pitchers appear to have noticed. Fastballs make up 61.2% of what he sees, above the 55.2% average and up every season, while his breaking-ball share is well below average. Offspeed is solid, with a 106.2 EV90 that ranks 22nd.

## Synthesis
Abreu wins by putting average-quality swings on a very efficient path. He swings at strikes and chases at league-typical rates and makes roughly average contact. Because he lifts so consistently, an unusually large share of his contact lands in the damage zone. That launch efficiency explains how a near-average EV90 and Hard-hit% produce top-quarter barrel and xISO numbers. It also means his expected batting average depends on contact frequency, which is why his strikeout reduction has pushed his xwOBA up three straight years. His defining strength is that lift-and-barrel skill, which also makes him dangerous against breaking balls, the pitch that beats many hitters. His biggest vulnerability is the fastball: he misses it more than most qualified hitters and has been chasing it more. Pitchers are already feeding him heaters and steering away from the spin he punishes.

The trend line has two readings. In 2026 his EV90 and Pulled Air% fell together, two of the usual markers of a slowing bat, and his Hard-hit% has declined two years running. Whiff% held steady, though, while Sweet spot% and opposite-field share rose and strikeouts kept falling. At 27, that pattern looks more like a deliberate trade of pull-side power for contact and all-fields lift than a decline. Still, his damage now rests more on angle than force. Further erosion in top-end exit velocity would test how much of the power remains.
