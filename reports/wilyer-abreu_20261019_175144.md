# Wilyer Abreu — RF, Boston Red Sox · Age 27 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Abreu is a fly-ball power hitter who wins with launch more than exit velocity. He lifts the ball far more than most hitters and turns an above-average share of it into barrels. His value comes from extra-base damage, supported by league-average plate discipline that has cut his strikeouts each season.

## Expected stats
His .354 xwOBA (house model; expected wOBA from contact quality, strikeouts and walks) ranks in the 73rd percentile and has risen in each of the last two seasons. He gets there through damage, not contact. His xBA (house model) of .252 sits just below the qualified average, while his xISO, the extra bases his contact should produce per at-bat, ranks in the 82nd percentile. His wOBA has trailed his xwOBA in both 2025 and 2026, which suggests his results understate his contact quality.

## Batted-ball profile
Abreu does his damage through efficient launch. His EV90 of 105.3 is only modestly above the qualified average. His 21.0-degree median launch angle is far above the league's 14.7, his launch spread is a bit tighter than average, and he keeps the ball off the ground. Together that produces a Barrel% in the 83rd percentile. He is not pull-dependent. His Pulled Air% (balls hit in the air to his pull side) is exactly league average and fell 6.3 pts this year, and his Pull% runs below average. His Sweet spot% and Oppo% rose alongside that drop, which reads as an all-fields adjustment rather than lost lift. The concern is exit velocity: EV90 fell 2.2 mph from 2025, and Hard-hit% has slipped two years running.

## Plate discipline
His approach sits near the middle of the league. In-zone swing rate, Chase% and Whiff% are all close to the qualified average, and his IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) is in the 54th percentile. The trend is the story. His K% has fallen from 28.0% in 2024 to 20.7%, now exactly league average, because his Whiff% dropped from its 2024 level and held there. He swung at fewer strikes in 2026, and that drove the dip in IZ Swing − Chase. His BB% is a touch above average.

## Platoon profile
He is an everyday bat, with an xwOBA of .359 against lefties and .352 against righties. He gets there differently on each side. Against left-handers he strikes out less but hits the ball softer and on the ground more. Against right-handers he does more damage, with a 106.0 EV90 and a higher xISO, at the cost of more strikeouts. His results gap between wOBA and xwOBA sits on the right-handed side.

## Vs. pitch type
He punishes spin, and fastballs are the hole. Against breaking balls his xwOBA ranks in the 92nd percentile, with a Barrel% in the 90th and a better-than-average Whiff%. He also hits offspeed pitches hard. Against fastballs, his Whiff% is in the bottom quarter of qualified hitters (20th percentile), his xwOBA is below average, and his Chase% has climbed each season since 2024. Pitchers appear to have noticed. His fastball share of 61.2% is above the 55.2% average and has edged up each year, while he sees fewer breaking balls than average.

## Synthesis
Abreu's value runs through launch. He swings and makes contact at roughly league-average rates. When he connects, though, the ball leaves at a productive angle, so a solid but unspectacular exit-velocity profile still produces barrels and near top-quarter expected power. That is how a hitter with an ordinary expected batting average posts an above-average xwOBA. The strikeout gains have amplified it: more balls in play gives the power more chances to show, and his walk rate has not suffered.

His biggest vulnerability is fastball contact. He misses fastballs more than most hitters, and pitchers lean on them while steering away from the breaking balls he punishes. The direction is mixed. His 2026 changes look like a deliberate trade of some pull-side power for steadier, all-fields contact: less pulled air, more line drives and opposite-field balls, fewer strikeouts. His xwOBA rose through that trade. The EV90 drop and the two-year slide in Hard-hit% are the signals to watch. His Whiff% has not risen with them, so at 27 this reads more as a dip in top-end exit velocity than a slowing bat.
