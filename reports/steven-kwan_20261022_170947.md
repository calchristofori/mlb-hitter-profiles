# Steven Kwan — CF, Cleveland Guardians · Age 28 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Kwan is an extreme contact-and-on-base hitter with almost no power. He rarely misses, rarely chases and repeats a line-drive launch better than anyone, but his exit velocity sits at the very bottom of the league. His value rests entirely on batting average and walks, which got him to a slightly below-average overall level in 2026 even as his damage kept shrinking.

## Expected stats
His xwOBA (house model), which strips the luck out of his results, was .332, just under the qualified average and a rebound from a weak 2025. The path there is lopsided. His xBA (house model) of .280 ranks near the top of the league, while his xISO, the extra bases his contact should produce per at-bat, was .067. That is second-worst among qualified hitters, and it has fallen two seasons in a row. The 2026 rebound came from contact and walks, not damage. His wOBA landed almost exactly on his xwOBA, so his results were an honest reflection of his skills.

## Batted-ball profile
Kwan pairs the most consistent launch in the league with the weakest exit velocity in it. His launch-angle spread was the tightest among qualified hitters, and his Sweet spot% of 41.4% was the best. He almost never tops or pops up the ball. But his EV90, the exit velocity of his hardest-hit balls, was 95.3 mph, last among qualified hitters. His Hard-hit% was also last, and both have fallen two seasons running, with a steep drop in 2026. He sprays the ball to all fields. His Pulled Air%, the share of balls he lifts to the pull side, fell to 12.9%, well below average. Rising sweet-spot and opposite-field rates suggest a deliberate all-fields approach, but that does not explain the lost exit velocity.

## Plate discipline
His contact skill is elite, and in 2026 he became passive in the zone. His Whiff% and Chase% were each the second-lowest among qualified hitters. He also swung at only 51.5% of pitches in the zone, against a qualified average of 66.8%. That passivity, not chasing, pushed his IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) into the bottom quarter of the league. Taking more strikes cost him little: his strikeout rate stayed among the league's lowest, and his BB% jumped to 13.3%, well above average.

## Platoon profile
Cleveland has played him every day. His share of PA against lefties has been well above the average for left-handed hitters in each of the last three seasons. Against lefties he whiffed more and did slightly less damage, posting a .320 xwOBA versus .337 against righties. His results against lefties also trailed his expected numbers. It is the same hitter on both sides with a modest gap, which makes him an everyday bat, not a platoon candidate.

## Vs. pitch type
Pitchers attack him with fastballs. Their share was 67.9%, the highest of any qualified hitter, and rose in 2026. His fastball contact is elite and his fastball xwOBA is near average. But his fastball EV90 and Hard-hit% were last in the league, and he swung at just 47.8% of fastballs in the zone. That reads as pitchers challenging him freely because he cannot punish strikes. Breaking balls and offspeed pitches bring lower returns without being a true hole. Offspeed is his weakest group at a .265 xwOBA, but pitchers throw it to him rarely, and he almost never whiffs on either group.

## Synthesis
Kwan wins by not giving away plate appearances. He rarely chases, rarely misses and almost always squares the ball up at a useful angle. That combination produces a high xBA, a low strikeout rate and, in 2026, a high walk rate. His defining strength is bat-to-ball skill paired with zone control, and it has held across all three seasons. His biggest vulnerability is the lack of damage. With the lowest exit velocity in the league, his line drives become singles, not extra-base hits. Pitchers know it, and they fill the zone with fastballs more than they do against anyone else. His growing tendency to take those strikes turned into walks this year, but it also suggests a hitter who sees little reward in swinging.

The trend is the concern. EV90 and Pulled Air% have both fallen two straight seasons, alongside Hard-hit%, so two of the three markers of a slowing bat are moving together. Against that, his Whiff% is steady, his sprint speed rose, and at 28 he is not at an age where decline is expected. Some of the change likely reflects an all-fields, take-first approach. Even so, the lost exit velocity leaves almost no margin. His value now depends on keeping his batting average high and continuing to draw walks.
