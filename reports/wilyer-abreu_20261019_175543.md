# Wilyer Abreu — RF, Boston Red Sox · Age 27 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Abreu is a launch-driven left-handed power bat. He hits the ball in the air far more than the typical hitter and turns that lift into barrels without needing elite exit velocity. His value comes from extra-base damage rather than batting average, backed by an approach that has climbed to league average and steadily trimmed his strikeouts.

## Expected stats
His xwOBA (house model; what his contact, walks and strikeouts should have produced) of .354 ranked 37th of 135 qualified hitters. It has climbed for three straight seasons, from .330 in 2024 and .347 in 2025. The shape is damage over contact. His xBA (house model) of .252 sits right at the .254 qualified average, while his xISO (the extra bases his contact should produce per at-bat) of .224 ranked 25th of 135, well above the .175 average. His .340 wOBA trailed his xwOBA, so his results modestly understate the quality of his season.

## Batted-ball profile
Abreu does his damage through launch rather than raw exit velocity. His EV90 of 105.3 is only modestly above the 104.6 qualified average. His 21.0-degree LA median sits far above the 14.7 average, though, and his LA SD of 27.1 is a bit tighter than average, so he repeats that lift. The result is a fly-ball-heavy mix and a 12.1% Barrel%, 24th of 135. In 2026 his direction changed. Pulled Air% (pulled fly balls, liners and popups as a share of batted balls) fell to 19.4% from 25.7%, landing exactly at league average. His Pull% dropped below average, while his Oppo% rose and his Sweet spot% climbed 4.4 pts to 36.3%. That combination reads more like a deliberate all-fields adjustment than a fade. The real flag is exit velocity: EV90 fell 2.2 mph from 107.5, and Hard-hit% has slid every season, from 51.1% in 2024 to 43.5%.

## Plate discipline
His approach is now league average nearly across the board, which is real progress. He swings at 67.6% of strikes and chases 29.5% of pitches off the plate, both near the qualified average. That gives an IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) of .381 against a .374 average. His Whiff% is close to average as well. The payoff shows in the results: K% has fallen from 28.0% in 2024 to 20.7%, exactly the qualified average, with an above-average 10.4% BB%. He did attack strikes less than in 2025, with IZ Swing% down 6.0 pts.

## Platoon profile
Abreu is an everyday bat, with a .359 xwOBA against lefties (217 PA) and .352 against righties (464 PA). He gets there differently from each side. Against left-handers he plays as a contact hitter, posting a .267 xBA and 16.6% K% but only a 103.0 EV90 and .195 xISO. Against right-handers the power shows up (106.0 EV90, .237 xISO), at the cost of a 22.6% K%.

## Vs. pitch type
The hole is the fastball. His fastball Whiff% of 23.7% ranks 108th of 135, in the bottom quarter, against an 18.8% average. His fastball xwOBA of .358 also trails the .368 average. Pitchers appear to have noticed. Fastballs made up 61.2% of what he saw, above the 55.2% average and up slightly each season since 2024, and his fastball Chase% has climbed from 18.2% in 2024 to 28.1%. Breaking balls are the reverse. He posts a .363 xwOBA (12th of 135) and a 14.2% Barrel% against them, with a Whiff% better than average, and pitchers throw him fewer of them than average (24.2% vs. 30.5%). He also handles offspeed well, with a .327 xwOBA (69th percentile) and an 84th-percentile EV90.

## Synthesis
Abreu's value rests on one dependable skill: he lifts the ball consistently enough that solid, not elite, exit velocity still produces a top-quarter barrel rate and xISO. Everything else in the profile is roughly league average. He swings at strikes and balls at normal rates, misses at a normal rate and makes ordinary amounts of contact, which is why his xBA is average. That middle-of-the-pack approach is itself a gain. The strikeout cuts since 2024 have let more of his launch-heavy contact count, and they are the main reason his xwOBA keeps rising even as his raw contact quality has not. He holds that level against both hands, so there is no platoon case.

The vulnerability is velocity in the strike zone. He crushes spin but swings through fastballs at a bottom-quarter rate, and pitchers are leaning into fastballs while he chases them more. The trend line deserves attention for the same reason. A lower EV90 and a three-year slide in Hard-hit% point to less top-end exit velocity, even though the drop in pulled air balls looks more like a spray adjustment than decline. At 27, that reads as a watch item rather than an aging signal. His launch is likely carrying more of the load than it did in 2024 or 2025, which leaves less margin if the exit velocity keeps slipping.
