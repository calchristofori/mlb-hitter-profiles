# Chandler Simpson — LF, Tampa Bay Rays · Age 25 (2026) · Bats L / Throws R

## Bottom line
Simpson is a slap-and-run hitter: elite bat-to-ball skill and top-tier speed, paired with almost the least impact in the league. He drives value by putting the ball on the ground and running, which keeps his batting average afloat despite almost no damage. Pitchers beat him by filling the zone with fastballs he can't punish, and his 2026 results ran well ahead of his contact quality.

## Who he is
Two things define him: he almost never misses, and he almost never hits the ball hard. His 10.8% Whiff% ranks 3 of 135 qualified hitters and his 7.8% K% ranks 2nd. His EV90 (90th-percentile exit velocity, a gauge of top-end power) of 96.6 sits 134 of 135, far below the 104.6 qualified average. His Barrel% was 0.0% in both seasons, with 0 home runs. The shape matches. His median launch angle is 1.5° against a 14.7° average, and 61.5% of his batted balls are grounders. His Pulled Air% is 7.1%, also 134th. That metric counts balls hit in the air to his pull side, where most extra-base damage happens.

This reads as a coherent profile rather than a broken one. With 29.5 ft/s sprint speed (6 of 135), grounders become hits. His xBA (house model) is .262, above the .254 qualified average. That is expected batting average from exit velocity and launch angle, with speed credited on grounders. His xSLG (house model) of .297 ranks last. The cost shows up elsewhere. His 4.2% BB% ranks 130 of 135, and his Chase% is above average. Together they leave his IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) in the 21st percentile. His value lives almost entirely in batting average.

His top-end exit velocity held steady, but his contact got worse. Hard-hit% fell 3.9 pts and Sweet spot% fell 4.5 pts. Line drives dropped from 27.7% to 21.6% while fly balls rose from 8.8% to 14.1%. That pulled his xwOBA (house model, expected wOBA) down to .263, 134 of 135. His actual .301 wOBA beat that by +.038, after the two matched almost exactly in 2025. His steady results likely overstate how well he hit in 2026.

## How pitchers beat him
Pitchers throw him fastballs and dare him to do damage, and they already lean on it heavily. Fastballs made up 63.4% of what he saw, against a 55.2% qualified average, the same diet as in 2025. He makes contact: his 5.5% fastball Whiff% ranks 2nd. Even so, his fastball xwOBA (house model) was .269, last among 135 qualified hitters across 416 PA ending on the pitch. He also expanded more against them, with his fastball Chase% jumping to 35.4% from 26.8%, in the 10th percentile. Results against breaking balls and offspeed also grade poorly. Those samples are small (117 and 76 PA ending), so they are tentative reads. His swing decisions against breaking balls are above average.

Left-handers are the other pressure point. Against them he posted a .229 xwOBA over 211 PA, versus .280 against right-handers, with a 1.4% BB%.

## What to watch
The key variable is contact quality, not bat speed. EV90 was flat at 96.7 and 96.6, so the 2026 drop came from fewer line drives and a wider launch spread, with LA SD up to 27.5 from 25.5. At 25, nothing here reads as decline. With no power to fall back on, though, whether that line-drive slip is noise or a new baseline decides how much his batting average can carry.
