# Wilyer Abreu — RF, Boston Red Sox · Age 27 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Abreu is a lift-and-damage hitter. His contact rate is about league average, but a high launch angle and frequent barrels make him one of the league's better extra-base producers, without elite exit velocity. In 2026 he became an everyday player: he held up against lefties and kept his strikeouts at a league-average rate.

## Expected stats
His .354 xwOBA (house model; what his contact, strikeouts and walks should have produced) ranked in the 73rd percentile, the third straight season it has risen. The value comes from damage, not average. His xBA (house model) sits just under the qualified average, while his xISO (expected extra bases per at-bat) of .224 ranked 25th of 135. His results trailed his expected numbers for a second straight season, so his surface line likely undersells his skill.

## Batted-ball profile
He is a fly-ball hitter who repeats his launch. His LA median of 21.0 sits far above the qualified average of 14.7, he hits few ground balls, and his launch spread is slightly tighter than average. His EV90 is only modestly above average, yet his Barrel% ranked 24th of 135, so his damage comes from efficient launch rather than top-end exit velocity. That changed somewhat in 2026. His EV90 fell from 107.5 to 105.3, and his Pulled Air% (balls in the air to the pull side) dropped from 25.7% to exactly league average. At the same time his Sweet spot% and opposite-field share rose. That pattern reads partly as an all-fields adjustment and partly as a side effect of facing many more lefties. The exit-velocity dip, though, also showed up against righties.

## Plate discipline
His discipline is now league average across the board, after a real improvement. His in-zone swing rate and chase rate are both within a point of average, and his IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) sits near the median. The gain is in contact: his Whiff% came down from 29.6% in 2024, and his K% fell from 28.0% to 20.7% over two seasons. His walk rate is above average. Two small cautions: his chase rate has crept up each year, and his in-zone aggression gave back its 2025 gains.

## Platoon profile
Boston shielded him from lefties in 2024 and 2025, when his share of PA against them ranked near the bottom among left-handed hitters. In 2026 that share jumped to 31.9%, well above the 25.1% average for lefty bats. He held up: his xwOBA was .359 against LHP and .352 against RHP. He is a different shape on each side. Against lefties he makes more contact and strikes out only 16.6% of the time, but with less exit velocity and more grounders. Against righties he is the power version (.237 xISO). He reads as an everyday bat.

## Vs. pitch type
Breaking balls are his best pitch group. His xwOBA and Barrel% against them both rank around the 90th percentile, with below-average whiffs, and he also does hard damage on offspeed pitches. His hole is fastball contact. His 23.7% fastball Whiff% ranked 108th of 135, and his fastball xwOBA is slightly below average. Pitchers have noticed. Fastballs made up 61.2% of what he saw, against a 55.2% average, a share that has edged up each season, while his breaking-ball share sits well below average.

## Synthesis
Abreu swings at ordinary rates, makes about average contact and puts the ball in the air at a high, repeatable angle. That combination gives him a middling xBA and a top-quarter xISO. His defining strength is converting contact into barrels, especially against spin, which pitchers now largely avoid throwing him. His biggest vulnerability is swinging through fastballs. When he connects with heaters he still barrels them at an above-average rate, but the misses keep his batting average ordinary and give pitchers a simple plan.

The trajectory is mostly positive. His xwOBA has risen three straight years, his strikeouts have fallen sharply, and he earned at-bats against lefties and produced against them. The flag is that his EV90 and Pulled Air% both fell in 2026, and his xISO slipped with them. At 27, with his Sweet spot% and opposite-field contact rising and far more lefty exposure, this looks more like a change in approach and role than a slowing bat. Still, his power leans on launch more than exit velocity, so whether his EV90 recovers is the key thing to track.
