# Wilyer Abreu — RF, Boston Red Sox · Age 27 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Abreu is a lift-and-barrel hitter whose value comes from extra-base damage, not batting average: he makes contact at a league-average rate and turns a consistent fly-ball swing into above-average power. In 2026 his strikeout rate fell to league average and he took on an everyday role against lefties, and his underlying performance rose for a second straight season.

## Expected stats
His xwOBA (house model, expected wOBA from contact quality, strikeouts and walks) was .354, comfortably above the qualified average. Power carries it. His xBA (house model) sits right at the league average, while his xISO (expected extra bases per at-bat) of .224 ranks well above average. xISO dipped from 2025, but xwOBA still climbed, so the gain came from somewhere other than damage on contact. His actual wOBA of .340 trailed his contact quality, mostly against right-handers, so his results undersell the bat.

## Batted-ball profile
Abreu wins through launch more than raw exit velocity. His median launch angle is well above the qualified average and his launch spread is a bit tighter than average, so he lifts the ball consistently. That shows in a 12.1% Barrel% that ranks well above average and has risen two seasons in a row. His exit velocity is only slightly above average: EV90 fell 2.2 mph from 2025 and Hard-hit% has fallen two seasons running. The bigger change is direction. Pulled Air% (balls in the air to the pull side) dropped to exactly the league average from 25.7% in 2025, when it was well above average. Pull% fell, Oppo% rose and Sweet spot% climbed, which reads as a hitter spreading the ball around rather than selling out to pull.

## Plate discipline
His discipline is average across the board: he swings at strikes slightly more than average, chases at the league rate and whiffs about as often as a typical qualified hitter. The progress shows in the results. His K% of 20.7% matches the qualified average and has fallen two seasons in a row from 28.0% in 2024, driven by a Whiff% that dropped from 29.6% in 2025 and stayed near that level. His BB% has also risen two straight seasons and now sits above average.

## Platoon profile
His role changed in 2026. After being shielded from lefties in 2024 and 2025, he faced them in 31.9% of his PA, more often than most left-handed hitters. He held up. His xwOBA against lefties (.359) was in line with his xwOBA against righties (.352), though he gets there differently: more contact and fewer strikeouts against lefties, more exit velocity and barrels against righties. He reads as an everyday bat, not a platoon candidate.

## Vs. pitch type
Breaking balls are his best pitch group. His xwOBA against them ranks 12th of 135, his Barrel% on them is near the top of the league and he whiffs less than average. Pitchers have noticed: almost no qualified hitter sees a smaller share of breaking pitches. Instead they feed him fastballs, 61.2% of his pitches, a share that has risen two seasons in a row. That is where the hole is. His 23.7% fastball Whiff% sits in the bottom quarter of qualified hitters, his fastball chase rate has climbed two straight years, and his results on fastballs are slightly below average. He handles offspeed pitches well.

## Synthesis
Abreu's value rests on lift. A consistent, elevated swing path turns moderate exit velocity into barrels and extra-base damage, while his league-average contact keeps his batting-average component ordinary. That is a damage-over-contact profile. What moved him forward in 2026 was the plate-appearance side: fewer strikeouts, more walks and the ability to handle lefties well enough to play every day. That raised his expected production even as his power on contact slipped from 2025.

His defining vulnerability is the fastball. He swings and misses at it more than most hitters, and pitchers are steering him toward it and away from the breaking balls he punishes. The trend line deserves a flag. EV90 and Pulled Air% fell together in 2026, and Hard-hit% has slid two years running. At 27, and with Sweet spot% and Oppo% rising, the direction change likely reflects an all-fields adjustment more than a slowing bat. Still, his exit velocity is now ordinary and the pulled-air power behind his 2025 damage has receded. He has become a more complete, lower-strikeout hitter, but his power is resting more on launch efficiency than on exit velocity.
