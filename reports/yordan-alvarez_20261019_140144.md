# Yordan Alvarez — DH, Houston Astros · Age 29 (2026) · Bats L / Throws R

## Bottom line
Alvarez was the most complete hitter among 2026 qualified hitters. He led all 135 in both xBA and xISO (house model), pairing elite exit velocity with a tight, lifted swing and a top-ten walk rate. Pitchers have no true hole to work. Their best option is offspeed stuff off the plate, which he chases more than most, and they are throwing it to him more every year.

## Who he is
He is a slugger who also hits for average. His .455 xwOBA (house model: what his contact, walks and strikeouts should have produced) ranked 1 of 135, built on a .320 xBA and a .330 xISO, both first.

The batted-ball shape explains it:
- **Force:** His EV90 of 110.0 ranks 6th, elite top-end impact.
- **Lift:** His median launch angle is 19.0°, against a 14.7° qualified average, and his GB% is 30.3% against 41.0%.
- **Consistency:** His LA SD of 24.5 ranks 4th, so few balls are wasted topped or popped up.
- **Direction:** Pulled Air% measures balls in the air to the pull side, where damage concentrates. His reached 24.2%, the 81st percentile.

The result is 15.1% barrels (5th) and 39.9% sweet-spot contact (6th).

The approach is patient rather than aggressive. His IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) of .348 sits at the 31st percentile. That reflects selectivity, not chasing: his 27.7% Chase% is better than average, while his 62.5% IZ Swing% is well below the 66.8% average. He misses little when he swings (19.9% Whiff%, 75th percentile) and walks 15.7% of the time, 6th in the league. At 25.4 ft/s (7th percentile), he adds nothing with his legs. The value is all bat.

## How pitchers beat him
Mostly, they don't. He ranked first in xwOBA against both fastballs (.496) and breaking balls (.443). He posted a 61.8% hard-hit rate on fastballs (3rd) and 19.0% barrels on breaking pitches (4th).

His relative soft spot is offspeed. His IZ Swing − Chase against changeups and splitters ranks 129 of 135. He chases 38.2% of them, just outside the bottom quarter, and takes in-zone ones more often than average. The damage thins out there: a .361 xwOBA and 9.4% barrel rate, both his lowest by pitch group, though the xwOBA still sits at the 92nd percentile. Contact isn't the problem, with a 20.5% Whiff% at the 87th percentile. Pitchers appear to have noticed. His offspeed share has climbed from 12.2% in 2024 to 17.2%, above the 14.4% average, while his breaking-ball share has fallen below average.

Left-handers are the other angle, though 184 PA is an early read. Against them his xwOBA fell to .420, from .468 against righties. He showed a softer EV90 (108.3), more grounders (37.2% GB%), more chasing (31.5%) and a far lower walk rate (8.2% vs 18.4%). Even so, .420 is well above the .338 qualified average. That is an everyday bat, not a platoon case.

## What to watch
A rising Pulled Air% alongside steady EV90 and Whiff% shows no sign of a slowing bat at 29; the power shape is sharpening. The thread to follow is the zone. His IZ Swing% has slid from 68.0% in 2024 to 62.5%, and his IZ Swing − Chase has fallen three straight seasons as pitchers feed him more offspeed. The key question is whether that patience keeps earning walks or starts conceding hittable strikes.
