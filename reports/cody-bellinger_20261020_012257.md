# Cody Bellinger — LF, New York Yankees · Age 30 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Bellinger is a contact-and-launch hitter, not a slugger. He rarely misses and lifts the ball efficiently to his pull side, which produces slightly above-average output driven by batting average rather than extra-base damage.

## Expected stats
His xwOBA (house model; expected wOBA based on contact quality, strikeouts and walks) of .340 sits just above the qualified average and has risen for two straight seasons. He gets there unevenly. His xBA (house model) of .270 ranks in the 73rd percentile, while his xISO (expected extra bases per at-bat) of .149 sits in the 34th percentile and fell from 2025. His results ran well ahead of his expected stats in each of the prior three seasons. In 2026 they matched, so this year's line is a truer picture of his skill.

## Batted-ball profile
His damage comes from launch efficiency, not exit velocity. His EV90 of 102.8 ranks in the 29th percentile, far below his early-Dodgers levels, and Hard-hit% and Barrel% both sit in the bottom third. The shape of his contact is excellent, though. His Sweet spot% ranks in the 91st percentile, his launch spread is tighter than average, and his Pulled Air% (balls hit in the air to the pull side) ranks in the 80th percentile. Without top-end exit velocity, that well-aimed lift turns into fewer barrels than its shape suggests. His LA median dropped from 20.0 to 16.0 this year, with fewer fly balls and more grounders. That flatter path likely explains why his xBA rose while his xISO fell.

## Plate discipline
Contact is his skill, and he is selective to the point of passivity. His Whiff% ranks in the 75th percentile and keeps his K% of 15.8% among the league's lower rates. His IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) ranks in only the 26th percentile, but chasing isn't the cause, since his Chase% is near average. The low mark comes from a below-average IZ Swing% that fell again in 2026. Taking more strikes came with an above-average walk rate, but it likely also means hittable pitches go by.

## Platoon profile
He played every day: 35.6% of his PA came against lefties, a 95th-percentile share among left-handed hitters and up from 2025. His contact holds against lefties, with the same xBA and even fewer whiffs. His power does not: his xwOBA is .315 against lefties versus .354 against righties, with a much lower xISO and barely any barrels. He is an everyday bat whose damage comes almost entirely against right-handers.

## Vs. pitch type
Breaking balls are his best pitch group. His xwOBA against them ranks in the 86th percentile, a turnaround from a weak 2024. Fastballs are a soft spot rather than a true hole. His fastball xwOBA and whiff rate are around average, but his fastball EV90 and Barrel% rank in the bottom quarter, and pitchers throw him slightly more fastballs than average. His results against offspeed pitches have been below average two seasons running (27th-percentile xwOBA, a tentative read on 93 PA). His whiff rate on them is good, so the problem appears to be soft contact, not misses.

## Synthesis
Bellinger's value is a chain. He swings less than most, chases at a normal rate and rarely misses, so many pitches end up in play. Because his launch is efficient and pull-oriented, a lot of that contact falls for hits, and his xBA climbs. But his exit velocity caps how far that contact travels, which keeps his xISO low despite a swing built for lift. His defining strength is that pairing of bat-to-ball skill and launch efficiency, which has held across four seasons. His biggest vulnerability is the missing exit velocity. It shows most against fastballs and left-handers, where he still makes contact but rarely drives the ball.

At 30, his EV90 has held steady, but his Whiff% and Pulled Air% both slipped in 2026 and his sprint speed dipped. The moves are small and come from strong starting points. Combined with a lower launch angle and a higher Sweet spot%, they read more like a deliberate shift toward line drives and average than like a slowing bat. Even so, the trade has cost him power, and two contact metrics moving the wrong way at once is worth watching.
