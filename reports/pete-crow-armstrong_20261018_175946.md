# Pete Crow-Armstrong: CF, Chicago Cubs, Age 24 (2026), Bats L / Throws L

## Bottom line
Crow-Armstrong is a top-tier power bat right now. His .383 xwOBA (house model) ranks 13 of 135 qualified hitters, and he leads all of them in Pulled FB% at 34.8%. Contact quality and batted-ball shape have improved for three straight full seasons. The limiter is approach: he ranks 108 of 135 in K% and chases breaking balls at a bottom-tier rate.

## Profile and role
**Data:**
- **Damage:** EV90 is 106.6, in the method's "good" band (105+) and above the 104.6 qualified average.
- **Launch:** LA median is 22.0 against a 14.7 average.
- **Batted-ball mix:** GB% is 31.3% against 41.0%, and Pull% is 54.9% against 44.1%.
- **Speed:** sprint speed is 29.5 ft/s, ranking 6 of 135.

**Interpretation:** He is a pull-side, air-ball power hitter with plus speed. The contact profile fits a middle-of-the-order run producer. The strikeout rate argues against hitting him at the top.

- His 2026 wOBA of .406 sits +.023 above his .383 xwOBA. That is below the .030 threshold.

- Interpretation: results flatter him somewhat, since his wOBA ranks 2 of 135 against an xwOBA rank of 13. BABIP jumped +.051 to .327, after .292 and .276 in prior seasons. That looks like a one-year spike, so read him as a .383-xwOBA hitter, not a .406-wOBA hitter.

**Vs. LHP:** the split does cross the threshold. In 230 PA he has a .387 wOBA against a .334 xwOBA, a +.053 gap.

Conclusion: speed is a partial explanation, but the gap is otherwise unexplained.

Interpretation: the more patient approach bought walks but also more called strikeouts.

- He does impact damage: xSLG is .516 (rank 9) and Barrel% is 12.9% (rank 15).

## Outlook
The trend is upward with no decline signals.
- EV90 rose +1.4 to 106.6.
- Whiff% fell −2.6 pts.
- Pulled FB% rose +4.4 pts.
- Pull% was essentially flat at −0.5 pts.

None of the method's slowing-bat markers are moving the wrong way, and at 24 none would be expected.

**The one thing to watch** is chase on breaking balls. If the 2026 walk gains hold and his 40.4% breaking-ball chase rate comes down, the strikeout problem eases. If not, pitchers have an obvious way to manage a bat whose contact is already among the best in the league.
