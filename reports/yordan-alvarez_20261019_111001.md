# Yordan Alvarez — DH, Houston Astros · Age 29 (2026) · Bats L / Throws R

## Bottom line
Alvarez was the best hitter in baseball in 2026 by contact quality, ranking first of 135 qualified hitters in xwOBA, xBA and xSLG (all house model, which grade each batted ball by its exit velocity and launch angle). He wins with elite impact delivered on one of the league's most consistent, lofted launch paths, and his value comes entirely from the bat. His soft spot is swing decisions against offspeed pitches, and pitchers are throwing him more of them each season.

## Who he is
He hits for average and power at once, with a .320 xBA and a .650 xSLG. His impact ranks near the top of the league on every measure. His EV90, the 90th-percentile exit velocity of his batted balls, is 110.0 (6th). His Avg EV is 94.4 (3rd) and his Barrel% is 15.1% (5th). What turns that force into results is the shape of his contact. His LA median of 19.0 sits well above the 14.7 qualified average, and his LA SD of 24.5 ranks 4th. Few hitters put the ball at a productive angle more consistently, so few of his batted balls are wasted. He rarely hits grounders (30.3% against a 41.0% average). His Pulled Air%, the share of balls hit in the air to his pull side where most extra-base damage happens, is a career-high 24.2% (81st percentile).

His approach is selective rather than aggressive. His IZ Swing − Chase (in-zone swing rate minus chase rate) of .348 is only 31st percentile. The low score comes from swinging at 62.5% of strikes against a 66.8% average, not from expanding the zone; his 27.7% Chase% is slightly better than average. He pairs that with a 19.9% Whiff% (75th percentile) and a 15.7% walk rate that ranks 6th. Together they suggest a hitter who waits for his pitch and accepts the walk otherwise. Speed adds nothing: his 25.4 ft/s sprint speed ranks 126th.

Every input is steady or improving except swing decisions: his power and miss rate have held while his pull-side lift has grown. His wOBA has trailed his xwOBA every season since 2021, including .434 against .455 in 2026, so even his league-best results likely understate his contact; park effects, a possible cause of a gap that persistent, are not in the data.

## How pitchers beat him
Mostly they don't. He ranks first in xwOBA against both fastballs (.496) and breaking balls (.443). Against breaking balls his swing decisions are in the bottom quarter, but his Barrel% on them (19.0%, 4th) more than covers it.

Offspeed is the relative weak point. His .361 xwOBA there still ranks 92nd percentile, but it is down from .506 in 2024. His swing decisions on offspeed are among the league's worst: his IZ Swing − Chase of .322 ranks 129 of 135. That comes from a 70.4% in-zone swing rate against a 76.4% average and a 38.2% Chase% that ranks 100th. He seldom misses when he does swing (20.5% Whiff%, 87th percentile), so the problem is pitch selection, not bat-to-ball. His .402 wOBA on offspeed also ran ahead of his xwOBA there. Pitchers appear to have noticed. His offspeed share has climbed from 12.2% to 15.6% to 17.2%, above the 14.4% average, while his breaking-ball share sits below average.

Left-handers cut his walk rate to 8.2% (18.4% vs RHP) and his xwOBA to .420 (.468 vs RHP). In a tentative 184-PA sample, that is still far above the .338 qualified average. He is an everyday bat.

## What to watch
The bat shows no aging signals at 29. His EV90 has held near 110 for three seasons, his Whiff% is back to 19.9%, and his Pulled Air% has risen each year. The trend to track is the steady slide in IZ Swing − Chase, especially if pitchers keep raising their offspeed share. His sprint speed fell to 25.4, which matters little for a DH whose value is all bat.
