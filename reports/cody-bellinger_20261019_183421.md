# Cody Bellinger — LF, New York Yankees · Age 30 (2026) · Bats L / Throws L

## Top line
Bellinger is a contact-and-launch hitter: he rarely misses, and he puts the ball in the air to his pull side at productive angles. Below-average exit velocity caps the damage, so his slightly above-average production comes through batting average rather than power.

## Expected stats
His xwOBA (house model; expected wOBA from contact quality, strikeouts and walks) of .340 sits just above the qualified average of .338. It has risen in each of the last two seasons. He gets there through contact. His xBA (house model) of .270 ranks 37 of 135. His xISO (expected extra bases per at-bat) of .149 sits in the 34th percentile and fell from 2025. His wOBA matched his xwOBA this year, after his results ran ahead of his contact in each of the three prior seasons, so 2026 is a clean read of his level.

## Batted-ball profile
Bellinger does damage through efficient launch, not exit velocity. His EV90 (90th-percentile exit velocity) of 102.8 ranks 96 of 135. That is well below his 2017–2019 levels, though it has held near this mark since 2022. His Hard-hit% and Barrel% both sit in the bottom third. What he does well is angle. His Sweet spot% of 38.7% ranks 13 of 135, his launch spread is tighter than average, and his Pulled Air% (pulled fly balls, liners and popups) ranks in the 80th percentile. The shape flattened in 2026: his LA median dropped to 16.0 from 20.0 and his FB% fell. That reads as a trade of some lift for line-drive contact, which fits the higher xBA and lower xISO.

## Plate discipline
His discipline rests on contact, not selectivity. His Whiff% ranks in the 75th percentile and his K% of 15.8% in the 77th. His IZ Swing − Chase (zone swing rate minus chase rate) falls in the bottom quarter. That comes from taking strikes, not expanding the zone: his IZ Swing% fell to 64.3%, below average, while his Chase% sits near average. The extra takes came with more walks, as his BB% rose to 10.5%.

## Platoon profile
He is the same contact hitter against both sides, with an identical xBA, but his power plays mainly against right-handers. Against lefties his xwOBA drops to .315 and his xISO to .117, with lower exit velocity and a Barrel% of just 3.2%. He still makes contact against lefties, with an even lower Whiff%. He reads as an everyday bat rather than a pure platoon candidate, though lefties hold him below average.

## Vs. pitch type
He has no clear hole by pitch group. Breaking balls are his best group, with an xwOBA that ranks 20 of 135. Fastballs show his limits. He rarely misses them, but he swings at fewer zone fastballs than average and chases them more. His fastball Barrel% ranks 113 of 135, leaving his fastball xwOBA a touch below average. Pitchers throw him fastballs at a slightly above-average share. His offspeed results were poor, a .202 wOBA in 93 PA. But his offspeed Whiff% ranks in the 78th percentile and his expected results ran well ahead of his actual results, so this looks more like a small-sample result than a hole.

## Synthesis
Bellinger wins by making contact often and wasting little of it. Low whiff rates put a lot of balls in play, and his consistent launch sends them to the pull side at sweet-spot angles. That combination produces an above-average xBA. What it cannot produce is impact. With exit velocity in the bottom third, his lift yields well-struck balls rather than barrels, so his xISO trails the league. The limit shows most against lefties and against fastballs, where he makes contact but does little damage.

His biggest vulnerability is that modest exit velocity, compounded by a willingness to take hittable strikes. The trend reads more like a change in approach than a slowing bat. His EV90 held steady while his launch angle came down and his xBA rose. Whiff% ticked up and Pulled Air% dipped this year, and both bear watching at 30. For now, though, both remain better than average, and his overall xwOBA keeps improving.
